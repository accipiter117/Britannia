# MILITARY / TACTICAL BATTLE

## Formations
Levies, Warriors, Skirmishers.
Cavalry deferred.

Per 100:
Levies: strength 1.0, food 10, wealth 2.
Warriors: strength 2.0, food 10, wealth 8.
Skirmishers: strength 1.2, food 10, wealth 6.

## Military capacity
Village 100 professional Warriors.
Town 300.
Major Town 600.
Warrior Halls/Fortifications can increase it.

## Recruitment
Recruitment removes population and workforce.
Example: recruit 300 Warriors → population -300, workforce falls, army +300.

## Army movement
Base 3 movement/season.
Road 1; Plains 1; Fertile 1; Forest 2; Hills 2; Marsh 2; Mountain 3; River crossing +1.

## Stances
Normal, Defensive, Aggressive, Forced March.

## Supply
Separate from upkeep.
Sources: friendly Districts, roads, depots, ports, foraging, captured territory.
States: Well Supplied, Adequate, Strained, Starving.

## Engagement
Adjacent hostile armies do not automatically fight.
When an enemy attempts to enter a District, defender can:
- Intercept
- Withdraw if possible
- Hold Position
- Ambush where terrain permits

## Tactical battle
Real-time with pause, 5–10 minutes, compact.
Approx. 12×12 logical battlefield.
Terrain: Open, Forest, Hill, River, Road.
Commands: Move, Attack, Hold, Retreat.

Battle objectives in prototype:
Field Battle: break enemy army.
Defensive Battle: hold objective until timer expires.

## Formation states
Advancing, Engaging, Holding, Retreating, Routing.

## Morale
0–100.
Affected by casualties, flanks, routing, commander loss, fatigue, supply, terrain, success and experience.
50+ normal; 30 shaken; 15 near breaking; 0 routed.
Routed formations cannot instantly return.

## Experience
Green → Seasoned → Veteran.
Veterans lose morale more slowly/recover faster/perform slightly better.

## Commander
Only a quality value: Poor, Average, Skilled, Exceptional.
Commander can die and generate Chronicle entry.
No character-management system.

## Combat
Combat intervals use:
attack power × formation effectiveness × terrain × morale × experience × commander.
Produce casualties, morale, fatigue, position.

Morale should generally break armies before annihilation.

## After battle
Apply casualties, morale, supply, experience, territory/control consequences.
Replacements can come from population after returning to friendly territory.
