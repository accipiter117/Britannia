# GAME DESIGN

Britannia is a dark, atmospheric historical strategy game about building and governing a kingdom in ancient Britain while surviving rival factions and major external pressures.

Tone: ancient, wet, swampy, beautiful, harsh, melancholy, morally ambiguous.

Philosophy:
**Britannia isn't a game about winning history. It is a game about making history.**

World hierarchy:
World → Region → District → Faction

District is the fundamental gameplay unit. Economy, population, buildings, culture, control, armies, supply and battles all operate around Districts.

Regions contain 4–8 Districts and provide broad strategic bonuses/status.

Map:
- irregular District polygons
- explicit neighbour connections
- slightly elevated 2.5D/diorama target
- approximately 30° camera
- soft borders
- banners/markers instead of giant coloured national borders

Three map layers:
1. Terrain
2. Infrastructure
3. Actors

Three zoom levels:
1. Strategic
2. Regional
3. District

District qualities:
- terrain
- population
- buildings
- development capacity
- resources
- infrastructure
- military presence
- political control
- culture
- loyalty
- prosperity

Control dimensions:
- Military Control
- Political Control
- Cultural Influence
- Religious Influence

Prototype need not show four dashboards.

Occupation:
Occupied → Administered → Integrated

Policies:
Extract / Autonomy / Integrate.

Culture changes slowly; conquest does not instantly assimilate populations.

Settlement:
Village → Town → Major Town.

Region status:
Presence → Contested → Majority → Dominant → Complete.

Seasonal loop:
LOOK → IDENTIFY → SELECT → DECIDE → ACT → CONSEQUENCE → CONTINUE → END SEASON → WORLD CHANGES → REASSESS.

One turn = one season:
Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter.

No rigid economic/military/diplomatic phases.

Historical events change the world permanently but do not force historical outcomes.

Chronicle records important events and generates a narrative campaign history.

Campaigns can continue after becoming a Major Power/Hegemon. Decisive Dominance creates an option to end the Chronicle rather than forcibly ending play.

Capital loss does not automatically end a campaign.
